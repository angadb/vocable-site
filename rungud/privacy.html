Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026. Rungud has not been released yet.

Rungud ("the App") is developed by Angad Singh Bedi ("I", "me"). This policy says what the App does with your data, and what it does not do.

The short version: I don't collect your data. The App reads your running and walking workouts from Apple Health, works out your training plan on your iPhone, and keeps it there. There is no account, no analytics and no server. Nothing leaves your iPhone unless you send it yourself.

Apple Health

With your permission, the App reads these from Apple Health:

  • Running and walking workouts, with their distance and heart rate
  • VO2 max

The App only reads. It never writes anything to Apple Health. It uses what it reads to show your runs and to build and adjust your training plan, and for nothing else. It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never shared with anyone.

Apple Health can tell the App when a new workout arrives, even when the App is closed, so that the plan can keep up. That happens on your iPhone, and the App does not send the workout anywhere.

You can change or remove the App's access at any time. Open the Health app, tap your picture, then Apps, then Rungud.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Your training plan and the answers you gave when you set it up. The plan is a file in a storage space on your iPhone that is shared between the App and its widgets, which is the App Group for my apps.
  • Your settings, such as miles or kilometres and your reminder time.
  • A short summary for the App's widgets (the next run, and the week so far), kept in the same shared storage space.

That shared storage space can be opened by the App, its widgets and my other apps on the same iPhone, and by nothing else. If you also install Gymgud, which I make, Gymgud can read your plan file and the widget summary on that iPhone to place your gym days around your runs. Nothing leaves the iPhone. Rungud 1.0.0 itself never reads anything Gymgud writes.

The App does not use iCloud and has no sync. Deleting the App deletes all of this. It does not delete anything from Apple Health.

Reminders

If you turn them on, the App sends you reminders on your run days. These are local notifications, made and scheduled on your iPhone. They do not come from a server.

Apple Watch

If you choose to, the App can send a planned run to your own Apple Watch. It hands the workout to Apple's Workout app, using Apple's own software on your iPhone, and Apple's Workout app takes it from there. The App itself sends nothing over the internet to do this.

Your numbers and feedback

The App keeps a few plain counts of how you use it, on your iPhone only: the date you first opened it, how many days you opened it, how many plans you made or removed, how many workouts you opened, how many times you copied or shared an export or opened the method page and the widget guide, and whether you are reading Apple Health and have a plan (yes or no only). These counts hold nothing read from Apple Health, such as distances, paces or heart rate, and the App has no way to send them. You can see them in Settings.

If you choose "Send feedback", the App opens an email to me in your own mail app. You write it and you send it. If you leave "Include in feedback emails" on, the counts are added to the email, where you can read, change or delete them before you send it. If you send it, I receive your email address and whatever the email contains. I use them only to read and answer your feedback.

Exports

You can export your runs as CSV or JSON, or as a text summary. Exports are made on your iPhone and go only where you choose to send them. If you paste a summary into another service, that service's own privacy policy applies to it.

No account, no analytics, no server

There is no account. The App makes no network requests of its own and no requests to any server of mine, and I have none. Your training plan is worked out on your iPhone from fixed rules: there is no AI model and no cloud service. The App contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools.

Apple gives app developers anonymous, combined statistics from people who have chosen to share them: how many people installed, opened or deleted the App, and crash reports. I can see those totals. I cannot see a person or a device. You control this on your iPhone in Settings, under Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements, Share With App Developers.

Some links open outside the App when you tap them, in your browser, the App Store or Apple's Health app and Settings: the privacy and support pages, and the page about my other apps. What those do is covered by their own policies. "Send feedback" opens an email in your own mail app.

Not medical advice

The App is a training aid, not a medical device, and its plans are guidance, not medical advice. If you have any health concerns, talk to a doctor before you start a training plan. If something hurts or feels wrong, stop and see someone.

Children

The App collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I will update this page and its date.
