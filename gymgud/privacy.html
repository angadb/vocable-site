Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026. Gymgud has not been released yet.

Gymgud ("the App") is developed by Angad Singh Bedi ("I", "me"). This policy says what the App does with your data, and what it does not do.

The short version: I don't collect your data. Your lift log lives on your iPhone, with a restore copy in your own iCloud. The App reads your running workouts and body weight from Apple Health and saves each finished session to it as a strength workout. There is no account, no analytics and no server of mine.

Apple Health

Only after you agree, in the App and then on Apple's own permission sheet, the App can:

  • Read your running workouts (their date, distance and duration), to suggest your gym days and to mark runs as done. This happens only if you ask for it in setup or in Settings.
  • Read your body weight, once at the start of a session, to show beside bodyweight lifts. The weight is copied onto that session in your lift log.
  • Save each lifting session you finish to Apple Health as a strength workout, with its start and end time. It saves nothing else: no sets, no heart rate, no calories.

It uses what it reads for those jobs and for nothing else. It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never shared with anyone. You can change or remove the App's access at any time: open the Health app, tap your picture, then Apps, then Gymgud. The App never deletes anything from Apple Health.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Your lift log: exercises, sessions, sets, your body parts and goal, units, and the body weight copied onto each session.
  • Your week: the answers you gave in setup, the days you pinned and the days you marked done.
  • A photo for an exercise, if you add one (see below).
  • Safety copies the App makes of your log, kept in its own folder on your iPhone. You can see some of them in the Files app, under On My iPhone, then Gymgud.
  • The end time of a rest timer you started, so it still runs if you leave the App.

Your iCloud copy

If you are signed in to iCloud, the App keeps a restore copy of your lift log, and of your exercise photos, in the App's own folder in your iCloud Drive. It uses iCloud Documents. It does not use CloudKit and I cannot see it. The copy is for a new phone or a reinstall: when the App opens with an empty log and finds a copy, it asks whether to restore it. It is not synced live between devices. Your week setup is not in the copy.

Deleting the App does not delete the copy. You can remove it in your iPhone's Settings, under your name, iCloud, Manage Storage. If iCloud is off, the log stays on the iPhone only.

Camera and photos

If you add a photo to an exercise, you can take one with the camera or pick one from your library. The camera is asked for only when you tap to take a photo. The App sees only the photo you pick, not your library. The photo is shrunk and kept with your lift log on the iPhone, in the iCloud copy, and in a backup file if you export one. It goes nowhere else.

Sharing the week with Rungud

Gymgud and Rungud, which I also make, can share your week through a folder on your iPhone that only my apps can open. If Rungud is installed, Gymgud reads its plan from that folder to place your gym days around your runs. Gymgud also writes a small file there with the gym focus planned for this week and next, and the App's version. It does not include your lift log. Nothing in that folder leaves the iPhone. Gymgud never changes Rungud's files.

Exports and backups

In Settings you can export your log as a spreadsheet (CSV), export a backup file that includes your photos, and import a backup. These use iOS's own save and open sheets, so the file goes only where you choose to put it. I never receive it. Importing a backup replaces your log after you confirm, and the old log is first kept as a dated copy in the App's folder on your iPhone.

Feedback

"Send feedback" in Settings opens an empty email to me in your own mail app. The App adds nothing to it: no version, no counts, no log. You write it and you send it. If you send it, I receive your email address and whatever the email contains, and I use them only to read and answer your feedback.

Links

The Privacy policy, Support and "Gud apps" rows in Settings open web pages in your browser, or show Apple's App Store card for another of my apps. That is Safari or the App Store talking, not the App.

No account, no analytics, no server

There is no account. The App makes no requests to any server of mine, and I have none. It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and it counts nothing about how you use it. It sends no notifications.

Apple gives app developers anonymous, combined statistics from people who have chosen to share them: how many people installed, opened or deleted the App, and crash reports. I can see those totals. I cannot see a person or a device. You control this on your iPhone in Settings, under Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements, Share With App Developers.

Not medical advice

The App is a training record and aid, not a medical device, and its suggestions are guidance, not medical advice. If you have any health concerns, talk to a doctor before you change how you train. If something hurts or feels wrong, stop and see someone.

Children

The App collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I will update this page and its date.
