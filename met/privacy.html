Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026. Minglegud has not been released yet.

Minglegud ("the App") is developed by Angad Singh Bedi ("I", "me"). This policy explains what data the App handles and how.

The short version: I don't collect your data. The App has no account, no servers and no analytics, and it makes no network requests of its own. What you note about people stays on your iPhone.

Data That Stays on Your Device

The following is stored locally on your device and is never sent anywhere by the App:

  • The people you add: names, notes, facts, dates and how you know them
  • The faces for them: pictures you chose or the App drew, and the descriptions you wrote
  • Your circles and your review history
  • Your app settings
  • Simple counts of how you use the App, such as cards reviewed and faces drawn

The App does not use iCloud and has no sync. "Erase everything" in Settings removes every person, circle, fact and review, and the counts. Deleting the App deletes everything it keeps.

Information About Other People

The App holds what you choose to write about people you know. It stays on your iPhone and I never see it. The App's widgets show a count of people, a count due for review, and the next person's first name and face or initials, never a note. The widget reads these from a small summary kept in a storage space shared only between the App and its widgets.

Contacts

If you tap "From Contacts", iOS asks whether the App may read your contacts. The App then lets you pick people and copies only the ones you choose: their name, nickname, birthday and photo. It does not copy the rest of your address book, and the copy stays on your iPhone.

Photos

To give someone a face from a photo, the App uses the system photo picker. It sees only the photo you choose, not your library. The photo is shrunk and kept with that person on your iPhone.

Microphone and Speech

The microphone is used only while you have tapped the microphone button. What you say is turned into text on your iPhone by Apple's speech recognition. The audio is not recorded or kept by the App, and neither the audio nor the text is sent to me. The first time, iOS may download Apple's speech language files to your iPhone. That is Apple's system doing it.

Apple Intelligence and Image Playground

On iPhones that support it, the App uses Apple's on-device language model (Apple Intelligence) to sort what you say or type into a card, and Apple's Image Playground to draw a face. When you draw a face, the description you typed is handed to Apple's Image Playground sheet. Both are features of iOS, and Apple's own privacy information covers them. The App does not send your notes or descriptions to any service of mine or to any third party. Without Apple Intelligence the App still works: you fill in the card yourself, and faces are your photos or initials.

Reminders

If you turn them on, the App schedules one reminder on your iPhone for 9 in the morning when people are due for review. It says how many, never a name. It is made by iOS on your device and no server is involved.

Birthday reminders are a separate switch in Settings, off until you turn it on. If you do, the App schedules a reminder on your iPhone at 9 in the morning, on the day or the day before, and that reminder includes the person's name. It is made by iOS on your device and no server is involved.

Counts and Feedback

The App counts a few things on your iPhone, such as days opened, cards reviewed and faces drawn. They are counts only, never a name or a note, and the App has no way to send them.

If you choose "Send feedback", the App opens an email to me in your own mail app. The email carries the App's version, your iOS version, whether Apple Intelligence is available, and those counts, where you can read, change or delete them before you send it. If you send it, I receive your email address and whatever the email contains, and I use them only to read and answer your feedback.

Network Requests, Analytics and Tracking

The App makes no network requests of its own. It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools. The page about my other apps in Settings opens in your browser or shows Apple's App Store card, which is your browser or the App Store talking, not the App.

Apple gives app developers anonymous, combined statistics from people who have chosen to share them: how many people installed, opened or deleted the App, and crash reports. I can see those totals. I cannot see a person or a device. You control this on your iPhone in Settings, under Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements, Share With App Developers.

Children

The App collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to This Policy

If this policy changes, I will update this page and its date.
