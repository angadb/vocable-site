Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026. Eatgud has not been released yet.

Eatgud ("the App") is developed by Angad Singh Bedi ("I", "me"). This policy says what the App does with your data, and what it does not do.

The short version: I don't collect your data. You photograph a meal, and the App names the foods on your iPhone with Apple's on-device model. The App has no account, no servers and makes no network requests. Your meals and photos stay on your iPhone.

How a photo is read

When you take or pick a photo of a meal, the App first looks for a barcode, using Apple's Vision framework on your iPhone. If there is none, it gives the photo, shrunk, to Apple's on-device language model, through Apple's FoundationModels framework, which is part of Apple Intelligence. The model names the foods and guesses a portion size in everyday terms. It is not asked for weights or calories. The App sends the photo to no server of mine or of anyone else, and has no code that could.

What Apple's software does underneath is covered by Apple's own privacy policy. If your iPhone does not support Apple Intelligence, or it is turned off, the App still works: you add the foods by hand.

The numbers come from a table of common foods stored inside the App, not from the photo and not from the internet. You confirm every food and portion before anything is logged. The numbers are estimates.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Your meals: the date, the foods, the portions, the calories, protein, carbs, fat and fibre, and any note.
  • A small copy of each meal's photo, if "Keep photos" is on. It is on by default. Turn it off in Settings to keep only the foods. The full-size photo is not saved by the App, and the App does not save anything to your Photos library.
  • Foods you add yourself, and the portion sizes you corrected, so the next one starts where you left it.
  • Your settings: units and your daily targets, which are off unless you turn them on.
  • Today's totals for the widget (calories, protein, carbs and fat, and your calorie target if you set one), kept in a storage space shared only between the App and its widget. It holds nothing about specific foods.

The App does not use iCloud and has no sync. Deleting the App deletes all of this.

Camera and photos

The camera is used only when you tap to take a photo of a meal. For photos from your library, the App uses the system picker, so it sees only the photo you choose, not your library. Neither is sent anywhere.

Erasing your data

"Erase everything" in Settings removes every meal, your own foods and your remembered portions. It leaves your settings and the widget totals, which go away when you delete the App.

Apple Health

The App does not use Apple Health. It reads nothing from it and writes nothing to it.

No account, no analytics, no server

There is no account. The App makes no requests to any server of mine, and I have none. It contains no analytics, advertising or tracking tools, sends no notifications, and has no feedback email, export or usage counts. The "Gud apps" row in Settings opens this website in your browser, or shows Apple's App Store card for another of my apps. That is your browser or the App Store talking, not the App.

Apple gives app developers anonymous, combined statistics from people who have chosen to share them: how many people installed, opened or deleted the App, and crash reports. I can see those totals. I cannot see a person or a device. You control this on your iPhone in Settings, under Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements, Share With App Developers.

Not medical or dietary advice

The App is a food log, not a medical device. Its numbers are estimates, and it does not tell you what to eat. If you have a health condition or an eating disorder, talk to a doctor or dietitian rather than relying on a log.

Children

The App collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I will update this page and its date.
